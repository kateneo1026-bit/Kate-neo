## Today's carousel · Hidden London: 5 bombed churches, one free walk

**Post:** 19:30 UK (01:00 in India/Pakistan) · **Location tag:** St Dunstan in the East (or "City of London") · Turn on the AI label if you use Gemini images.
**Best option:** if you can be in the City today or this weekend, **take the photos yourself** (phone, daylight, straight on). Real photos of real places beat AI for this audience. Gemini prompts below are the backup.

**Facts (checked):**
- **Christ Church Greyfriars:** Wren church, hit by fire bombs on 29 Dec 1940. Only the tower and some walls survived. The nave is now a rose garden: the hedges mark where the pews were and the tall wooden frames mark the pillars.
- **St Alban, Wood Street:** Wren's tower is all that survived the Blitz. It stands alone in the middle of the road and is now a private house.
- **St Mary Aldermanbury:** Wren church gutted in 1940. In the 1960s its stones were shipped to Fulton, Missouri (USA) and rebuilt as a memorial to Churchill. In London, only a small garden on the old foundations is left.
- **St Dunstan-in-the-East:** bombed in 1941; Wren's tower and the walls survived; a public garden since 1971, with trees growing inside.
- **All Hallows by the Tower:** the City's oldest church (founded 675). Blitz damage uncovered a Saxon arch built with reused Roman tiles. Samuel Pepys climbed its tower in 1666 to watch the Great Fire. Free to enter.
- Walk: St Paul's station → Tower Hill station, about 2.5 km, ~40 min with stops (approximate). Gardens close at dusk (about 4pm in winter).
