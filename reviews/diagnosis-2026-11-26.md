## What went wrong: 4 causes, most important first

### 1. The topic was global, so Instagram found a global audience
Notre-Dame + bees + a fire is interesting to *everyone on Earth*. Instagram tests a reel on people who watch similar clips (animals, famous landmarks, "amazing facts"), and the biggest, cheapest pool of those viewers is India and Pakistan. The reel then went to **Explore** (26%), which spreads it even wider.
The parrot and the king's door were **London-only stories**, and they went to **71% UK**, aged 45+. Instagram matched them to London lovers.
👉 **The topic picks the country.** A story that's only useful to people who can go there reaches people who can go there.

### 2. The bees reel then "taught" Instagram the wrong audience, and St Dunstan paid for it
After 869 people in South Asia watched the bees, Instagram's guess about "who likes Kate's reels" moved towards them. The next reel (St Dunstan) was tested on a similar group. A quiet London garden means nothing to them, so they scrolled away and Instagram stopped the test at **46 views**, under the usual ~120.
That's why St Dunstan did worse than the parrot, even with a better hook and better prompts. **You didn't do anything wrong in that reel; the reel before it moved the test group.** This fades after 2–4 reels that UK people watch and save.

### 3. The VPN is still on in every screenshot
Instagram uses your **IP address**, phone settings and posting history to decide which country your account "belongs to" and where to test your reels first. With the VPN on, your account may look like it's in another country every day. On 24 Nov we said to turn it off; it's still on (all 20 screenshots).
👉 **Turn the VPN off for Instagram completely** (or exclude Instagram in the VPN's "split tunnelling" settings) for at least 2 weeks. Post, comment and browse from your real UK connection.

### 4. Possible: Meta AI translation
The bees reel was the first one with an English voiceover (ElevenLabs). Instagram can offer to **auto-translate and dub reels with Meta AI** (including into Hindi). If that was on, Instagram would show a Hindi version in India.
👉 Check: open the bees reel → ⋯ → look for **"Translations" / "Meta AI translations"**. Turn it off. Also check it before sharing each new reel (Advanced settings).

**Also contributing (smaller):**
- **AI-made scenes of real places.** London-lovers know what St Dunstan looks like. An AI version with an "AI info" label is easy to skip. For real London places, **your own phone footage beats Flow**. Flow is still great for the parts nobody can film (1941, a fire, a parrot in 1926).
- **The profile doesn't convert:** 32 visits, 0 follows. Check the name and bio fix from 24 Nov is done.
- **Followers who never watch:** follower views fell 60%; 2 people unfollowed. Most followers are follow-backs (see 24 Nov). Don't follow-for-follow.

## The solution: the "UK anchor" plan

### A. Today (10 minutes)
1. **VPN off** for Instagram (split tunnelling or off).
2. **Meta AI translations off** on the bees reel and in your reel settings.
3. **Don't delete** the bees reel and **don't boost** it. Deleting doesn't reset anything; boosting teaches Instagram the wrong audience even more.
4. Don't answer spam DMs or follow back new accounts from the bees reel (often bots).
5. Profile: name `Kate · Hidden London & Europe`, bio `Hidden stories behind famous places 🗝️ / London & Europe · free walks, quiet corners ☕🤎`. Pin: warships, parrot, king's door (UK audiences), **not** the bees.

### B. The "UK anchor" rule for every post (from today)
Before a topic is accepted it must pass **two tests**:
1. **Send test** (as before): *"Would someone send this to a friend with 'look at this'?"*
2. **Go-there test (new):** *"Could a person in the UK go there this month?"* The post gives them a reason to act: a free place, a walk, a station, opening hours, a Eurostar city.

And every post carries **local signals** Instagram can read:
- A **UK location tag** (the exact place, e.g. "St Dunstan in the East"), never "Paris" alone.
- **Words on screen and in the caption that only locals use:** the Tube station, "free", "5 minutes from Tower Hill", "a Saturday walk", British spelling.
- **UK-specific hashtags** (#londonwalks #cityoflondon #londonhistory #secretlondon), no global ones like #bees #paris #history.
- **CTA = save or send for a plan** ("Send this to the friend you'd do this walk with") instead of "Comment 🐝" (global audiences comment emoji; that's what Instagram read as success).
- **Europe stories, framed for UK travellers:** "2h15 from London by Eurostar", "a weekend from Stansted". Never a global landmark + an animal on its own.

### C. Train the algorithm towards the UK (15 minutes a day, 2 weeks)
- Comment on **5 UK accounts** a day: London history, London walks, London cafés, UK heritage (National Trust–style places, City churches). Real comments, 5+ words.
- Save and send UK London-history reels you like (your own "For you" feed follows what you do).
- Ask 3–5 **UK friends** to watch, save and send in the first hour.
- When possible: a **Collab post** with a small UK account (a London walking guide, a café, a local history page). This is the fastest way to reach their UK followers.
- If Instagram offers you **Trial Reels** (non-followers only), use them to test a hook without hurting your follower signals.

### D. Format: real footage for real London places
- If you're in London (the West End outing this week): **film the places yourself** on your phone: 4 clips × 5s, locked camera, one movement (leaves, a pigeon, a door). No AI label needed. This is also "original content", which Instagram now ranks higher.
- Flow / Gemini only for the past (1666, 1941, the 1920s) and for cosy home scenes.

### E. Timing
Post at **19:30 UK** (= 01:00 in India/Pakistan, when they sleep). Avoid 12:30 UK on weekdays: that's 17:00–18:00 in Pakistan/India, their prime time.
