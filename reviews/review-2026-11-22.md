## 2. What worked

- **The two warships reel (14 Nov)** is still the clear winner: 431 views, more than 3× any other post, plus the most likes (9), 2 saves and 1 share. Why: a **real, visible object with an impossible origin** ("a shop built from warships"). People can picture it in one second, and it's a named London place.
- **The king's door reel (19 Nov)** didn't score high, but it held people the longest: **7s average watch**, against 2s for the photo collage. A moving Flow video with one strange story works better than still photos.
- The slow-morning collage tops the score table only because of **one share**. With a 2s watch time and 85% skips, it isn't a format to repeat.

## 3. Repeat

1. **Another "impossible object" hidden story in a named London spot** — the warships formula. Monday's police station in a lamp post (formula 11) fits it exactly.
2. **Flow videos that show the strange thing in the first second**, bright and moving (the king's door kept people 7s; ~70% still left by 3s, so the payoff must come even sooner).
3. **Keep the routine:** post at 19:30, then comment on 6 niche posts and stay online for 30 minutes.

## 4. Stop

1. **Static photo collages as reels** (2s watch time). Lifestyle reels must move: steam, hands, a curtain.
2. **Vague places.** "Somewhere in Europe" was the weakest post (88 views). Always name the street, city and tag the location.
3. **Ending without a reason to follow.** 56 people visited the profile and almost nobody followed. Tell them what they get: "a new hidden London story every Monday, Wednesday and Friday".

## 5. Test next week

**A numbered series: "Hidden London · No. 1, 2, 3…"** on the reel cover and the first caption line, and the bio changed to *"Quiet days and hidden stories · a new hidden London place every Mon/Wed/Fri"*. A series gives visitors a reason to follow. Measure it by follows and profile visits next Sunday.
