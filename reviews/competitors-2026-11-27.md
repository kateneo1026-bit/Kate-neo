## 5 patterns
1. **The place looks unreal in frame 1, with the sound off.** Nobody needs to read anything (same as our Flow rule 20).
2. **"The real-life [famous fiction]"** (Hogwarts, Avatar) gets the most **sends**. The send reason is "this looks like X, let's go".
3. **Small, elegant serif text**, 3–7 words, centred: a title plus the place name. Calm and premium, which suits the brown, cosy look better than big bold captions.
4. **Real footage:** a slow walk or a drone, with one smooth movement, in daylight or golden hour. No voiceover needed.
5. **High saves = a place you can go.** People save it for a trip.

## How we use this (with the 26 Nov "UK anchor" rule)
- **The topic still has to pass the go-there test** (somewhere a UK viewer can visit). A global topic brings a global, passive audience (bees reel: India 54%).
- **New hook formula 34:** "London's real-life [famous fiction]": famous fiction + a real UK place + one impossible fact.
- **Title style:** a small serif title + 📍 the place name, on a soft dark band (Flow rule 21).
- **Film real places yourself** (faceless: the place only, no people as the subject). Use Flow only for the past.
- Then/now suits Flow well: past scenes in Flow, today in your own footage.
